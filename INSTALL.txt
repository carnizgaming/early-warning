EARLY WARNING - desktop dashboard
=================================
A live early-warning board for where you live in the UK or the US: official alerts, weather and
wind, floods and tides, wildfires (US), power cuts, the grid, local roads and news, world
flashpoints, disease outbreaks, food recalls, the pound or dollar, space weather and more.
Free public data only.
It runs quietly in the system tray and pops up a notification if something serious happens.

FIRST TIME: enter your UK postcode or US ZIP code. It works out your weather warning area,
flood, river and tide gauges, power data and local news. You can fine-tune what counts as "near you" and which roads to watch.

WINDOWS
  Early-Warning-Setup-1.0.6.exe     installs it (Start menu + desktop shortcut)
  Early-Warning-Portable-1.0.6.exe  runs without installing (e.g. from a USB stick)
  Windows will say "Windows protected your PC" because the app isn't signed by a paid
  certificate. Click "More info" then "Run anyway". It only asks the first time.

MAC  (Apple silicon M1-M4: ...mac-arm64.zip   Intel Macs: ...mac-x64.zip)
  1. Unzip, and drag "Early Warning" into Applications.
  2. Open Terminal and paste this line once (the app isn't signed with an Apple certificate):
       xattr -cr "/Applications/Early Warning.app" && codesign --force --deep -s - "/Applications/Early Warning.app"
  3. Open it from Applications. If macOS still blocks it: System Settings > Privacy & Security > Open Anyway.

LINUX
  Early-Warning-1.0.6.AppImage
  Make it executable (right-click > Properties > Allow executing, or: chmod +x Early-Warning-1.0.6.AppImage)
  and double-click it. Some distros need libfuse2 installed for AppImages.

USING IT
  - Closing the window keeps it running in the tray; right-click the tray icon to quit.
  - The tray dot is green / yellow / amber / red with the overall level.
  - Click any panel title (or its expand button) to open it full screen. Esc closes.
  - Settings (top right) changes location, feeds, notifications and start-with-computer.

PRIVACY
  Your postcode is only sent to postcodes.io once (a US ZIP code to zippopotam.us and the
  National Weather Service once), to look up the area. After that the app only downloads
  public feeds. Nothing about you is uploaded anywhere.
